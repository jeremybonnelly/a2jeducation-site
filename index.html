Improving access‑to‑justice education in Canada

Every year, many people in Canada face legal problems they cannot resolve. Legal professionals can help close that gap, but only if their education gives them the tools to build a people-centred, A2J-focused practice.

Launched in September 2025, the Access to Justice Education Initiative (A2JEI) is a national project led by the BC Access to Justice Centre for Excellence at the University of Victoria and McGill University’s Faculty of Law, with generous funding from the CBA Law for the Future Fund.

Research report, September 2026

Building the A2J Lawyer

Access-to-Justice Competencies and Educational Pathways in Canada

This report identifies the access-to-justice competencies Canadian lawyers need, from plain-language communication and trauma-informed practice to Indigenous and Reconciliation competencies. It then maps where law students can develop them today.

Between February and July 2026, we reviewed more than 500 courses and 133 clinical and experiential programs across all 24 Canadian law faculties, along with more than 13 law society competency frameworks, and consulted legal educators across the country.

Our report identifies current strengths and potential avenues for enhancing access-to-justice education in Canada.

A free, online introduction to access to justice

You’ve heard of the access-to-justice crisis, but you’ve never had a chance to learn its causes, its consequences, and what you can do about it? Introduction to Access to Justice is a free, self-paced course built for Canadian law students and practitioners, and open to anyone who is curious about this topic.

Each module combines short video lectures, curated readings and self-graded quizzes. It is free, online, available in English and French, and you can take it at your own pace.

Land acknowledgement

The Access to Justice Education Initiative carries out its work across lands now called Canada, which have been cared for by First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples since time immemorial and continue to be governed by their laws. We acknowledge these enduring relationships, and we recognize that meaningful access to justice includes respect for Indigenous legal orders and self-determination.
